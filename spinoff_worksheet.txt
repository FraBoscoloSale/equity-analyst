Spinoff Worksheet

Purpose:
Evaluate one company from the spinoff screen.
Screens to look at, not a recommendation.

1. Facts
Record:
- Parent
- Distribution date
- Distribution ratio
- Record date
- Price and price date
- Market value
- Index
- Window end date
- Rank and factor points

Every fact must state its filing, section, and date.
Mark each financial figure as:
- historical
- pro forma
- reported after the spin

If a required fact cannot be established, write "not known".

2. Why It May Be Cheap
Use the Investment Advisor persona.

Determine whether forced or indifferent selling can explain why the company may be cheap, or explain why this is not that story.

Use only filing-supported facts, the calculated value range, and the price against the low case.

State what evidence in the next filing would change the view.

3. Value Range

Owner cash flow:
After-tax earnings + depreciation - capital spending

Print every input and the arithmetic.

Valuation method:
Owner cash flow / required return

Low case required return: 12%
Mid case required return: 10%

Low case value:
Owner cash flow / 0.12

Mid case value:
Owner cash flow / 0.10

Compare the current price with the low-case value.

No model computes a number.

4. What the Parent Pushed Into It
Record:
- Debt raised
- Where the cash went
- Liabilities left with the company
- Restrictions left by the parent

Give an amount, filing, section, and classification for every item.

If the relevant sections were checked and nothing was found, state:
"looked in [sections], found none"

Never estimate a missing amount.

5. The Parent After the Spin
Record filing-supported facts needed to understand the parent after the distribution.

Every numerical fact must include its filing, section, and date.

6. Insiders and Catalysts
Count only open-market insider purchases reported on Form 4 with transaction code P.

Do not treat other Form 4 transaction codes as open-market purchases.

Record:
- qualifying insider purchases
- buybacks
- restrictions
- relevant filing-supported catalysts

7. Can You Buy It

Client purchase size: $100,000

Calculate median daily dollar volume from the closes and volumes supplied by the reader.

Compare the client's $100,000 purchase size with median daily dollar volume.

State whether the position can reasonably be purchased at the stated size based on that comparison.

The liquidity line is added in code after the model call.

8. View and Conviction

The view must state whether the value indicated by the filing evidence is more or less than the current price.

State the one number that decides the view and give its filing or line.

Conviction rule:

HIGH:
- low-case value is at least 1.25 times the current price; and
- no input required to calculate the low case is "not known".

LOW:
- otherwise.

If the close is "not available", the price comparison and view are "not computed".

9. Not Known
List every required fact that could not be established from the available filings or reader data.

Never estimate a missing fact.
